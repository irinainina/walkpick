WalkSpot

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 8, 2026

WalkSpot is an Android app that records your walks and the plants, birds and places you photograph on the way. It is made and run by Irina Inina, an individual developer based in Ukraine. This page says what the app keeps about you, where it goes and how to remove it.

What WalkSpot keeps

  • Account. Your email address. If you sign in with Google, also your first name as Google gives it.
  • Profile. Sex, date of birth, height and target values, if you enter them. They are used to count calories, stride length and a healthy weight range.
  • Walks. Start and end time, duration, steps, distance, calories, the route as a line of GPS points, the name of the walk and your note.
  • Photos and finds. The photos you take in the app, where and when each was taken, and the name the app or you gave it.
  • Health entries. Mood, weight, blood pressure and glucose values that you type in yourself.
  • Settings. Units, reminder time and days.

WalkSpot has no advertising and no analytics or tracking libraries. Your data is not sold and not shared for marketing.

Location, steps and camera

  • Location is read only while you record a walk, take a photo or look at the map. A walk keeps recording with the screen off; a notification is shown the whole time. Outside a walk the app does not follow you.
  • Steps are read from the phone's step sensor between Start and Stop of a walk only.
  • Camera is used only when you open it in the app. WalkSpot does not read your phone's gallery.

Where it is stored

Everything is kept on your phone and copied to your account on Supabase, a hosting service, on servers in the European Union (Ireland). Photos lie in private storage that only your account can read. The copy lets you sign in on another phone and get your history back.

Services that receive some data

  • Supabase — stores your account and data and sends the sign-in code to your email.
  • Google — signs you in if you choose "Continue with Google", and draws the map (Google Maps). Google receives the map areas you look at.
  • Pl@ntNet — a photo you mark as a plant is sent there to be identified. It goes without your name, email or location.
  • Wikipedia — is asked for articles about places near a point on the map and for descriptions of finds. The request carries coordinates or a name, not your account.
  • Open-Meteo — is asked for the weather at your location to show it on the Today screen and to name a walk. The request carries coordinates only.

Birds are identified on the phone itself; those photos are not sent to an identification service.

How long it is kept and how to delete it

Your data is kept until you delete it. You can delete a single walk, photo or health entry in the app. Settings → Delete account removes your account, all your data and all your photos from the server for good. You can also ask for deletion by email.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, for a correction or for deletion, and you can withdraw the permissions for location, physical activity, camera and notifications in the phone's settings at any time. Write to the address below; you will get an answer within 30 days.

Health information

WalkSpot is not a medical device. The numbers and remarks it shows are for your own reference and are not medical advice.

Children

WalkSpot is not meant for children under 16 and does not knowingly keep their data.
